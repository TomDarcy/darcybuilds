Where to start

You know the work.
Which parts could AI help with?

AI is moving quickly, but working out where it fits around production, engineering and quality takes more than trying a chatbot. Many smaller manufacturers don’t have someone internally who can assess the options and build the useful ones.

The starting point is usually already there: RFQs in email, engineering documents on a shared drive, inspection records, spreadsheets and information in your ERP. I look at where people repeatedly read, search, compare, copy or summarise it.

Sometimes the right answer is a clearer workflow, a database or a conventional application. The aim is to choose the approach that makes the work easier. If no opportunity justifies a build, I’ll explain why and what needs to change first.

What happens

From the factory floor
to a sensible first pilot.

  1. Understand the business

    A short initial conversation establishes the teams, goals and scope. During the factory visit, I speak with the relevant operations, engineering and quality staff.

  2. Map the work

    We walk through actual workflows and review the spreadsheets, systems, documents and handovers involved. I look for repeated effort and the exceptions that make the work difficult.

  3. Find and assess opportunities

    I aim to identify around 5–10 opportunities, then assess their value, feasibility and risks. Each is considered against both AI and simpler software approaches.

  4. Define the first pilot

    We work through the strongest option: the problem it solves, what the pilot should do, how we’ll judge whether it helps and an indicative build budget. You leave with the decisions and open questions needed to scope the work.

Recognisable manufacturing work

The kinds of opportunities we look for

These are starting points to investigate, rather than a promise that every process needs AI.

RFQs & quoting

Extract quantities and requirements from customer documents, prepare a quotation draft or compare supplier quotations for someone to review.

Engineering knowledge

Help engineers find relevant SOPs, manuals, earlier jobs, drawings and decisions, with links back to the source material.

Inspection & quality

Draft inspection plans from customer requirements, summarise NCRs or explore patterns in inspection records. Quality staff review and approve the output.

Documents into data

Turn emails, PDFs and certificates into structured records, with checks for missing details and exceptions that need human attention.

Shift notes & reporting

Summarise handover notes and production records, or automate the recurring reports that keep someone copying information every morning.

Work instructions

Prepare draft instructions from approved engineering information, then route them to the people responsible for checking and releasing them.

The focus is supporting people with information and administration. Autonomous machinery control and safety-critical production decisions are outside this service.

Example pilot brief · fictional scope and budget

Turn one RFQ format into a checked draft record.

Problem and value: Quote preparation starts with someone retyping customer requirements. Reduce that manual work and give the quoting team a consistent starting point.

What it should do: Extract part references, quantities and requested dates from one agreed RFQ format. A person checks and corrects the draft before exporting it. Direct ERP integration and automatic quote approval are outside this first pilot.

How we’ll judge it: Compare handling time and corrections against the current process using representative RFQs. Agree the success measures before building.

Indicative build budget: £5k–£8k. This fictional example assumes one document format, a review screen and a simple export. It is a planning estimate, not a fixed quote.

To confirm during scoping: Sample documents, field variations, data access and the export format. These determine the final scope, price and delivery plan.
