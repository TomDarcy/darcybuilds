Case study / Precision manufacturing

An inspection form is only
the start of the workflow.

I built a digital inspection system connecting engineering requirements, shop-floor measurements and usable quality data. Engineers defined what to measure; operators recorded the results during production.

Environment
Precision manufacturing
Users
Quality engineers + operators
Focus
Inspection, SPC & alerts
  1. 01Engineer defines inspection
  2. 02Operator records measurements
  3. 03SPC, alerts & quality data

Anonymised project. All interfaces are representative reconstructions with fictional measurements and part details; they are not original production screenshots.

01 / Define the inspection

The engineering definition comes first.

Quality engineers define the characteristics, nominal values, upper and lower specification limits, inspection frequency and measurement requirements.

02 / Capture at the point of work

A clear, simple view for the operator.

The operator receives the defined inspection and records the measurements during production. Large controls and clear pass/fail feedback keep the task focused on the part in front of them.

03 / Use the data

Measurements that feed the quality workflow.

Structured records can feed SPC charts, trend monitoring, out-of-tolerance alerts and historical analysis. The measurements stay connected to the inspection rather than disappearing into paperwork.

The connected workflow

Designed for both sides of inspection.

The engineering team needs a precise definition of the measurement. The operator needs a simple way to capture it. Connecting those two views creates structured data for quality monitoring and process analysis.
